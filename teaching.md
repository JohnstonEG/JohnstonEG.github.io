## Instructor of Record

**ECO 138: Economic Reasoning Using Statistics**
Illinois State University, Spring 2026 (one section) and Fall 2026 (two sections, about 65 students)

An undergraduate course covering descriptive statistics, probability, sampling distributions, hypothesis testing, and regression, applied to economic data and decision-making.

**Course redesign.** I rebuilt the course around real case studies, each following the same six steps:

1. Introduce the question.
2. Explain the statistical method.
3. Present the case.
4. Apply the method to it.
5. Show what the original analysts actually did.
6. Close on the limitations of the published analysis.

Ending each case on its limits is deliberate: the goal is for students to judge evidence, not only compute it.

**Homework.** Assignments are mastery-based and autograded, with numbers that regenerate on each attempt, so students can practice a method until they have it rather than memorizing one answer.

**Program assessment.** I designed and scored the course's program-assessment instrument: two hypothesis-testing problems spanning one- and two-sample inference under known and unknown variance, scored against a three-point rubric for 26 students and reported to the department for program review.
